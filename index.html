The Domestication of Fire
Anthropology ~700 words Difficulty: ★★★★☆

The control of fire represents one of the most transformative milestones in human evolution, yet pinpointing precisely when our ancestors first harnessed it remains a matter of considerable debate. Archaeological evidence from sites such as Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa suggests that hominins may have controlled fire as early as one million years ago, though many anthropologists argue that habitual, deliberate fire use did not emerge until roughly 400,000 years ago.

The distinction between opportunistic and habitual fire use is crucial. Early hominins almost certainly encountered naturally occurring fires—those sparked by lightning strikes or volcanic activity—and may have salvaged useful tools or food from the aftermath. This passive engagement, however, differs fundamentally from the active cultivation of flames: keeping a fire alive, transporting embers, and ultimately generating fire through friction or percussion.

Once mastered, fire revolutionized nearly every dimension of hominin life. Cooked food, as Richard Wrangham has argued, dramatically increased caloric availability and digestibility, freeing energy that could be redirected toward brain growth. The shrinking of the human gut and the proportional expansion of cranial capacity over the past 1.8 million years coincide suggestively with this dietary shift. Furthermore, communal cooking may have laid the social foundations for the family hearth—a focal point around which language, storytelling, and cultural transmission could flourish.

Fire also extended the productive day. Whereas other primates retire at dusk, hominins gathered around flames could continue toolmaking, planning, and social bonding well into the night. Cognitive psychologists have speculated that this extension of waking hours, combined with the protective warmth and predator deterrence fire provided, accelerated the development of complex social cognition.

Yet the archaeological signature of early fire use is notoriously elusive. Charcoal can be deposited by natural wildfires; reddened sediment may result from sun exposure rather than combustion. To distinguish anthropogenic fire from natural occurrences, archaeologists rely on a constellation of indicators: charred bone fragments arranged in patterns, burnt lithics suggesting in-place heating, and stratigraphic evidence that flames were confined to specific locations within a habitation site.

Recent micromorphological techniques have allowed researchers to identify ancient hearths with unprecedented precision. At Qesem Cave in Israel, for example, scientists discovered a 300,000-year-old hearth used repeatedly across generations, the soil chemistry preserving a record of sustained, controlled burning. Such findings reinforce the view that, by the Middle Pleistocene, fire had become a stable and central feature of hominin existence.

The implications stretch beyond biology into the realm of cognition and culture. Mastery of fire required foresight—gathering fuel before nightfall, anticipating wind direction, teaching the next generation. It demanded cooperation and trust: a fire too close to flammable shelter could be catastrophic. In this sense, the hearth may have been not merely a source of warmth but the original classroom, the first laboratory, and the earliest theater of human imagination.

LECTURE · BIOLOGY
Symbiosis in Coral Reefs
教授講解珊瑚與蟲黃藻的共生關係,並討論氣候暖化對此平衡的破壞。練習辨識「cause-effect」結構。
~ 2 min
顯示講稿 · Transcript

Today I want to talk about one of the most striking examples of symbiosis in the natural world — the relationship between coral and a microscopic algae called zooxanthellae. Most reef-building corals contain millions of these tiny algal cells within their tissues. The arrangement is mutually beneficial. The algae provide the coral with sugars produced through photosynthesis, which can account for up to ninety percent of the coral's energy needs. In return, the coral provides the algae with shelter and access to sunlight near the ocean surface. However, this delicate partnership is highly sensitive to temperature. When water temperatures rise even one or two degrees above normal, the coral expels the algae in a stress response known as bleaching. Without their algal partners, corals lose their vivid color and, more critically, their primary food source. Prolonged bleaching often leads to coral death. This is why even modest increases in ocean temperature, driven by climate change, pose an existential threat to reef ecosystems worldwide.

LECTURE · ART HISTORY
The Bauhaus Movement
介紹包浩斯學院的設計哲學與其對現代主義建築的影響。注意專有名詞拼法與年代序列。
~ 2 min
顯示講稿 · Transcript

The Bauhaus was a German art school that operated from nineteen nineteen to nineteen thirty three. Founded by the architect Walter Gropius in the city of Weimar, it sought to unify fine art, craft, and industrial design under a single educational philosophy. The school's core belief was that good design should be both functional and accessible to ordinary people, not just the wealthy. Bauhaus designers favored simple geometric forms, clean lines, and industrial materials like steel, glass, and concrete. Decoration for its own sake was rejected. This aesthetic — often summarized by the phrase "form follows function" — became enormously influential. When the Nazi government forced the school to close in nineteen thirty three, many Bauhaus faculty members emigrated, particularly to the United States. They brought their ideas to institutions like Harvard and the Illinois Institute of Technology, shaping modernist architecture in cities like New York and Chicago throughout the twentieth century.

LECTURE · ASTRONOMY
Exoplanet Detection Methods
凌日法與徑向速度法的比較。練習「compare-contrast」題型的筆記策略。
~ 2 min
顯示講稿 · Transcript

Today we'll compare the two main techniques astronomers use to detect planets around other stars — what we call exoplanets. The first is the transit method. When a planet passes in front of its host star from our line of sight, it blocks a tiny fraction of the star's light. By measuring this periodic dip in brightness, astronomers can infer the presence of the planet, estimate its size, and even calculate its orbital period. NASA's Kepler space telescope used this method to discover thousands of exoplanets. The second technique is the radial velocity method. As a planet orbits its star, its gravitational pull causes the star to wobble slightly. This wobble shifts the wavelength of the star's light — toward red when the star moves away, and toward blue when it moves toward us. By measuring these shifts, astronomers can determine the planet's mass. Each method has limitations. Transits only work if the planet's orbit aligns with our viewpoint, while radial velocity favors larger planets close to their stars. Most confirmed discoveries today rely on combining both techniques.

07 — STRATEGY

實戰 心法

考前一個月的衝刺重點。從時間分配到考場心態,六條原則助你穩定發揮。

01
不要為一題糾結

閱讀每題平均約 90 秒。若一題思考超過 2 分鐘仍無頭緒,標記後跳過。寧可放棄一題,不可拖垮整篇。

02
聽力先抓骨架

講座前 30 秒幾乎一定包含主旨。專注聆聽教授使用 "Today we'll look at..." 或 "The key idea here is..." 等訊號句。

03
口說模板靈活用

模板是骨架不是肉。"In my opinion" 可換成 "I tend to believe" / "It seems to me that"。考官會聽出生硬背誦。

04
寫作先框架後填肉

前 3 分鐘用 bullet points 列大綱:立場 + 兩個理由 + 各自的具體例證。寫作中思路不會卡頓。

05
考前 48 小時減量

不要臨時抱佛腳。考前兩天降到每日 1 小時複習,重點是睡眠與作息調整。大腦在飽和狀態下無法精準反應。

06
心態 ≥ 實力

考試焦慮會吞噬 5–10 分。練習時就模擬真實環境:戴耳機、開計時器、不喝水。讓考場成為熟悉的場景。
